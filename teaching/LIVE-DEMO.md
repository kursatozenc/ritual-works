# Ritual Works student onboarding demo

Use this short demonstration after students have created a ChatGPT or Claude
Project and added the two Ritual Works materials.

## Before class

1. Create a project called **Culture Design Field Studio — Demo**.
2. Paste `Student-Project-Instructions.txt` into its Project Instructions.
3. Upload `Designing-Organizational-Culture-Project-Guide.md` as a project
   source or file.
4. Start one blank chat inside the project.
5. Keep the fictional notes below ready to paste. Do not use a student’s live
   partner-organization material for the first demonstration.

## Eight-minute run of show

### 1. Show the persistent setup — 1 minute

Open the Project Instructions and Team Guide. Explain:

> We add these once. Every new conversation inside this project can use the same
> team. We still choose one teammate at a time.

### 2. Choose a teammate — 1 minute

Start the blank project chat with:

> Work with me as the Ethnographer. I want to make sense of interview notes.

Point out that the student chose the seat; the AI did not silently assemble a
team or choose a method.

### 3. Use a safe fictional case — 2 minutes

Paste:

> These are fictional, anonymized notes from three interviews about a new
> employee’s first week.
>
> - New employee: “I did not know which questions were okay to ask my manager.”
> - Buddy: the checklist was complete, but they added a private message at the
>   end of each day asking what still felt unclear.
> - Manager: “The onboarding page covers everything,” but described two times
>   the employee waited until the weekly meeting to raise a blocker.
> - New employee: said the buddy’s end-of-day message made small questions feel
>   easier to ask.
> - Unknown: whether other new employees had the same experience.

Ask for an observation brief. Notice whether the Ethnographer distinguishes
reported evidence, repeated moments, interpretation and unknowns.

### 4. Demonstrate the human checkpoint — 2 minutes

Correct or question one part of the brief. Then say:

> Do not move to design yet. What evidence is still missing?

Use this to show that a polished answer is not automatically a sound answer.

### 5. Open a second chat — 1 minute

Return to the same project and start another chat:

> Which teammate should help me pressure-test a possible onboarding practice?

The answer should recommend the Challenger and wait for the student to choose.
This demonstrates that the project remembers the team while separate chats stay
focused.

### 6. Close — 1 minute

Leave students with three rules:

1. Choose one teammate for the work in front of you.
2. Remove names and use only material you have permission to share.
3. Treat the output as a draft to question, correct and discuss with people.

## What to watch for

- If the AI begins designing during the Ethnographer chat, remind it to remain
  in the selected seat and stop at the human decision.
- If it calls one quote a pattern, ask it to show the supporting moments and
  count.
- If students are not ready to create a Project, let them use one standalone
  teammate prompt in an ordinary chat. They can still participate fully.

## Contribute and coach

The teammates are built to contribute, not to quiz. When they have enough
context, they offer a draft, a direct answer or a reasoned recommendation,
explain the evidence and uncertainty behind it, then ask one focused question
so the team can critique it. The rhythm is **contribute → invite critique →
let people decide**.

They ask for more information only when it would change the work, and they
never assume evidence, consent, an agreed owner or trial results. Each seat
contributes differently:

- **Ethnographer:** organizes supplied evidence and drafts plausible
  interpretations or framings for people to challenge and choose between.
- **Designer:** offers three concrete possibilities and their tradeoffs for
  people to choose, revise or reject.
- **Challenger:** gives a reasoned critique and recommendation, then asks which
  concern needs a response.
- **Experimenter:** drafts a feasible plan once prerequisites are confirmed, or
  interprets actual results with their limits.

Ask for more scaffolding when you want to learn the method, or for a draft when
you want direct help. Either way, the teammates never fill the human checkpoint
or choose the direction.

### Check the balance during rehearsal

With the fictional notes and role already supplied, the Ethnographer should
produce a concrete evidence brief before asking a focused evaluation question.
Ask “What is an insight?” and check for a direct explanation and example.
Then ask “Which framing should we choose?” and check that it offers a reasoned
recommendation while leaving the selection to students. These are rehearsal
checks to run, not completed behavioral test results.
