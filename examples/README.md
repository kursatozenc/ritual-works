# A small example

This fictional example shows the shape of the journey. It is intentionally
short; real work should contain richer notes and more human correction.

## What the team lead brings

> During three Monday planning meetings, the facilitator asked, “Any risks?”
> The room was silent each time. In two of those weeks, engineers messaged the
> project lead after the meeting about delivery concerns. In the third week, a
> concern appeared in the project channel that afternoon. I do not know why
> people waited. Roles present: project lead, designer and four engineers.

## The Ethnographer

**Observed:** The risk question was asked in three meetings and received no
spoken response. Concerns appeared later in all three weeks.

**Reported:** No participant explanation was provided.

**Inferred:** The meeting may not feel like a workable moment for raising risk.
This is an interpretation, not an established cause.

**Unknown:** Whether silence came from power, timing, ambiguity, lack of risk or
another factor.

**Possible recurring moment:** “Risks move from the shared meeting into private
or delayed channels.” Count: three.

The team lead corrects the brief and chooses that moment. The AI does not make
the selection silently.

## Is a small practice the right response?

A small practice might help change when risks become visible, but it will not
solve consequences for speaking up if those exist. The team lead decides to
continue while gathering feedback about power and safety.

## One of the Designer's three ideas

### Write, pass, discuss

- **When:** Immediately after the existing risk question.
- **Who:** Everyone already in the planning meeting.
- **Steps:** Take 45 seconds to write privately. Each person shares one risk or
  says “pass.” The facilitator asks which item needs action today.
- **Time:** Four minutes total.
- **Repeatable phrase:** “Write, pass, discuss.”
- **Ending:** One owner is named for each risk discussed, then planning resumes.
- **Choice:** Passing is spoken without explanation.
- **Replaces:** The open-ended risk question and silence that follows.
- **Evidence connection:** Concerns currently appear after the meeting.

The team lead may select this idea, modify it, combine it with another or reject
all three.

## The Challenger

The central risk is that saying “pass” may still be conspicuous, especially for
junior employees. Anonymous writing would reduce exposure but create a new
tool, while silent individual writing followed by the facilitator collecting
only volunteered items preserves the no-new-tool constraint.

**Recommendation:** CHANGE BEFORE TRYING.

**One change:** The facilitator asks for volunteered risks after writing; no
round-robin response is required.

**Learning test:** Stop if participants continue moving concerns into private
messages and report that the meeting version feels unsafe.

The team lead decides whether that change is sufficient.

## The Experimenter

The Experimenter cannot finish the trial plan until a real group agrees, a
person explicitly agrees to own it, a review date is scheduled and the team
chooses one count plus one unprompted signal.

When those are confirmed, it prepares the plan and stops:

> The next step happens with people, not with AI. Run the trial and return with
> notes about what actually happened. I will not create results in advance.

There is no fictional happy ending in the example. The next page can only be
written from a real trial.
