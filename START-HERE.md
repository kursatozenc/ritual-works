# Start here: work with your culture design team

You do not need to know anything about agents, prompts, Markdown or coding.
Add the team to a ChatGPT or Claude Project once, then choose the teammate you
need in each conversation. You lead the decisions.

## Before you begin

Bring notes from a real situation you observed. Rough notes are better than a
polished diagnosis. You might bring:

- bullet points from several meetings;
- an anonymized transcript;
- notes from interviews or conversations;
- a description of repeated moments, including dates or counts when known.

Before sharing, remove names and identifying details. Do not paste confidential
employee information, performance records, health information or anything that
could put a person at risk. Make sure you have permission to use the material.

If all you have is a conclusion such as “people do not trust leadership,” the
Ethnographer should help you identify what to observe next rather than pretending
the conclusion is evidence.

## Set up your team once

1. Create a new Project in ChatGPT or Claude.
2. Copy the short
   [`Project Instructions`](docs/downloads/Project-Instructions.txt) into the
   Project Instructions field.
3. Upload the
   [`Culture Design Team Guide`](docs/downloads/Culture-Design-Team-Guide.md) to
   the Project's files, sources or knowledge area.
4. Start a new conversation inside the Project and choose one teammate:
   Ethnographer, Ritual Designer, Challenger or Experimenter.

The setup stays available in future conversations inside that Project. The AI
uses one teammate at a time and stops whenever you need to make a decision or
do something in the real world.

If you do not want to create a Project yet, copy one complete prompt from
[`prompts/teammates/`](prompts/teammates/) into an ordinary conversation. It
will work for that conversation without any setup.

## What will happen?

### 1. The Ethnographer helps you see

The Ethnographer separates:

- what happened;
- what people said;
- what appears repeatedly;
- what happened only once;
- what is still unknown;
- what the group already does well.

You review this account and choose one recurring moment to focus on. The
Ethnographer does not choose it for you.

### 2. You decide whether a ritual fits

The team asks whether a small repeated practice could help, or whether the
underlying issue requires a change in workload, authority, staffing, incentives,
policy or leadership. It is valid—and sometimes responsible—to stop here.

### 3. The Designer offers possibilities

The Designer creates three small practices connected to something the group
already does. You can choose one, combine or change them, ask for different
ideas, or reject all three.

### 4. The Challenger conducts a reality check

The Challenger asks about burden, awkwardness, power, consent, accessibility
and what would show the idea is not helping. It recommends one of:

- **Ready to try**
- **Change before trying**
- **Do not try this idea**

You make the decision.

### 5. The Experimenter prepares a short trial

The Experimenter helps you name the group, secure an owner, choose a review
date and decide what to notice. Then the conversation stops.

### 6. Real people try it

The AI cannot perform this part. The group tries the practice, changes it,
ignores it or rejects it. All of those outcomes are information.

### 7. You return with observations

When you have actual evidence, start or return to an Experimenter conversation
inside the same Project. The Experimenter helps distinguish what was planned
from what happened and whether the practice should continue, change or end.

## Your role as team lead

The teammates can organize evidence, suggest possibilities and question
assumptions. They cannot witness your workplace, understand every relationship,
secure consent or take responsibility for the consequences.

Your job is to:

- protect the people described;
- correct unsupported claims;
- make the important choices;
- invite affected people into the decision;
- stop when the practice is inappropriate;
- report what really happened rather than what you hoped would happen.

You are not a customer receiving an answer. You are a member of the design
team.
